## 1. Real vehicles on their published masses

Every propellant load, empty mass, thrust and Isp is the published one, and so are the payload and the orbit; only the flight is this tool's. A real vehicle reaches its published orbit with nothing to spare, so the speed in hand should be near zero. A margin is the simulation's error and the data's together, and the two cannot be separated: 100 m/s is about 1 % of the speed to orbit, and about the size of the uncertainty in most of these published masses. Fairing and adapter masses are this tool's own estimates, because the sources rarely give them. Nothing was adjusted.

## 3. Saturn V (book pp.393-395)

The book's Figs. 6.35 and 6.37 show a lofted ascent whose angle of attack rises to 10-14 deg in the S-II burn. Flown here: S-IC burnout at 162 s and 64 km, and an angle between thrust and velocity of up to 17 deg in the S-II burn. The real S-IC and S-II shut their centre engines down early, which this model does not.
